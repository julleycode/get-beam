## the thread

**1. i'd stop saying "founders" and pick one painfully specific person.**

"founders" is not a market. "solo devs who just shipped a saas and have no clue how to get users" is.

you can't find 100 of everyone. you can find 100 of someone.

i'd write that one person down and only talk to them.

**2. i'd go where 100 of them already gather. not build an audience from scratch.**

most broke founders burn month one farming twitter followers. wrong order.

your first 100 customers are posting today, somewhere. subreddits. discords. reply guys under bigger accounts.

i'd find the 5 rooms they live in and show up daily.

**3. i'd do things that don't scale, on purpose.**

automation is for when you already know what works. right now you don't.

i'd dm 10 people a day, by hand, no template, referencing something they actually said.

30 real conversations a week beats 3000 cold emails into the void.

**4. i'd treat the traffic i already have as my warmest channel.**

here's the part nobody says out loud:

people are already visiting your site. they click your launch post, read, and leave. most of them vanish. no email, no signup, just a bounce.

that's not dead traffic. that's your hottest list, walking out the door.

so i'd install beam, see exactly who those visitors are, find their handle, and say hi on their feed in my own voice.

someone who already looked at your product is warmer than any cold name you could scrape. you just have to catch them before they forget you.

**5. i'd turn every conversation into content.**

every dm, every objection, every "oh that's clever" is a post.

i'd reply in public more than i post. replies put you in front of an audience that already exists. posting into your empty feed does not.

**6. i'd sell the outcome, not the feature.**

nobody buys "ai visitor enrichment." they buy "see who checked you out and ghosted, then win them back."

i'd lead with the result and the feeling. every single time.

**7. i'd make saying yes brainless.**

free to start. no card. cancel in two clicks.

every step between curious and trying it costs you a customer you already earned. i'd kill friction before i add features.

**8. i'd chase the people who said no.**

the ones who bounced know exactly why your thing isn't landing.

i'd hunt the no's harder than the yes's. that's your roadmap and your messaging fixes, handed to you for free.
