# beam launch video, scripts for X

Goal: drive waitlist signups. You on camera. English, beam lowercase voice. Fun, bold, laser gun in hand. Three formats, three second hook on each, built around how X actually ranks video in 2026.

---

## first, the honest tension you need to decide on

You asked for 1:20 to 2:00. The 2026 X algorithm disagrees, and you should know that before you shoot.

Native video gets the strongest push in the feed right now, but watch completion is a direct ranking signal and the biggest boost goes to video that holds people to the end. Under 60 seconds is where completion rates stay high. A 90 second video that 30 percent of people finish will travel less far than a 45 second video that 70 percent finish. (Sources at the bottom.)

My take, committed: shoot the hero version at 80 to 100 seconds because a launch deserves a real moment, then cut a 35 to 45 second version from the same footage and make THAT the one you push on launch day. The long one lives on your site, your pinned tweet, and LinkedIn. The short one farms the For You feed. Every script below is written so the first 45 seconds is a complete, postable video on its own. You lose nothing by cutting.

If you only shoot one, shoot the short one.

---

## what the X algorithm rewards in 2026 (the version that matters for this video)

This is the cheat sheet the scripts are engineered against, not a lecture.

Native video beats everything. Upload straight to X. Do not link to YouTube or Loom. Native video is currently the most boosted content type in the feed.

Watch completion is the lever. The first three seconds decide whether anyone reaches the value. Hooks below are built to stop the thumb cold. Aim for over 50 percent completion, which earns extended reach.

Replies are worth far more than likes. Current weighting treats a single reply as roughly worth dozens of likes. So every script ends on a question or a take that begs an argument in the replies, not a polite "check it out."

The first hour is the whole game. Engagement velocity in the first 30 to 60 minutes is the single strongest signal. Post when your founder audience is awake and online, reply to every comment for that first hour, and treat it like a launch, not a post.

No links in the post itself. External links cut reach by 50 to 90 percent. The waitlist link goes in your first reply, never in the main tweet. This is non negotiable and the playbook below handles it.

Conversation starters win. Hot takes, opinions, and questions get pushed harder than announcements. None of these scripts say "we are launching." They pick a fight with the status quo instead.

---

## production north star (applies to all three)

Energy: you are the most confident person on the timeline. Talk slightly too fast. Cut every pause. Bold, a little unhinged, never corporate.

The laser gun is the literal beam. That is the whole reason it works. Every time you fire it, something anonymous turns into a real person on screen. Visitor row lights up. Ghost becomes a face. Blurry profile snaps into focus. The prop is the metaphor. Use it as punctuation, not decoration. One great laser moment beats five throwaway ones.

Captions burned in, always. Most people watch muted. Big, bold, lowercase text, one line at a time, synced to your delivery. The video has to land with zero sound.

Voice stays lowercase beam. dry, playful, a little cheeky. never hype words like "revolutionary" or "game changer." talk like a founder texting another founder.

Two hard rules from your brand guardrails: do not claim a specific match rate on camera. no "60 to 80 percent." say "most of them" or "who they actually are." And no fake dashboards with fake names of real people. use obviously demo data or your own real beam screen.

Shot list to capture once, reuse across all three: you walking in, you firing the laser at the lens, you firing at a screen, a clean screen recording of the beam dashboard, you reacting to a "ghost," a tight close up for the closing line.

# format 1: the hot take to camera

The sharpest one for the algorithm. It is you, a take people will argue with, and the product as the proof. Conversation starter built to farm replies. This is the one I would push hardest on launch day.

Why it wins: opinion plus founder face plus a clean demo in the middle. Highest reply potential of the three.

### three second hook
Visual: hard cut straight to your face, mid sentence, no intro, laser gun already in frame resting on your shoulder.
Line: "you got 500 visitors today and talked to exactly zero of them."
Caption slams on: **500 visitors. zero conversations.**

### beat sheet (target 85s, first 40s is the cutdown)

0:00 to 0:03, hook above. No logo, no "hey guys." Straight into the accusation.

0:03 to 0:12
"that is normal, by the way. 97 percent of the people who visit your site leave without a trace. no email, no signup, no form. just gone."
Caption: **97% leave no trace.**
Action: you shrug, almost bored, like this is a crime everyone has accepted.

0:12 to 0:22
"everyone told you the fix is more traffic. it is not. you do not have a traffic problem. you have a 'you have no idea who these people are' problem."
Caption: **not a traffic problem.**
Action: lean into the lens on the last line.

0:22 to 0:40 (this is the demo, the heart of it)
"so. beam." *you fire the laser gun at a screen beside you*
Cut to the dashboard. A row lights up exactly where the laser hits.
"this is who is on your site right now. their name. their company. the page they are reading. and their latest post on x."
Captions, one per beat: **see them.** then **find them.** then **say hi on their feed.**
Action: the moment the laser hits the screen, the anonymous row resolves into a real profile. That is the hero shot of the whole campaign.

0:40 to 0:58
"not a cold email that dies in spam. a reply. on their turf. in your voice. beam writes the draft from their actual posts, you read it, you hit send from your own account. thirty seconds."
Caption: **not a cold email. a hello.**
Action: you mime hitting send, the laser gun does a little spin.

0:58 to 1:18
"i built this because i was tired of watching people bounce and doing nothing about it. it is in private beta. spots are limited and i onboard every single person myself."
Caption: **private beta. limited spots.**

1:18 to 1:25, the close and the reply bait
"tell me i am wrong. what is the last visitor you actually talked to? reply, i am reading every one."
Caption: **what was your last real visitor convo?**
Final frame: you point the laser gun at the lens, fire, screen cuts to the beam wordmark.

CTA on screen, last 2 seconds: **beam. see who's into you. say hi back.** (link goes in your first reply, not here.)

# format 2: the ghost skit

The fun, bold, high production one. You play it half straight, half ridiculous. This is the one people repost because it made them laugh, which is its own distribution.

Why it wins: visual gag in the first second, shareable, and the laser gun gets its best moment here. Lower reply rate than format 1, higher repost rate.

### three second hook
Visual: you at your desk refreshing analytics. Behind you, out of focus, a person draped in a white sheet (a literal ghost, lean into how cheap it looks) stands up and starts walking toward the door.
Line: you, without turning around, "hey. hey, where are you going?"
Caption: **POV: a visitor is leaving your site.**

### beat sheet (target 90s, first 45s is the cutdown)

0:00 to 0:05, hook above. The ghost ignores you and keeps walking.

0:05 to 0:15
You spin your chair around. Now you see three or four "ghosts" wandering out of frame.
"these are my website visitors. five hundred today. and every one of them is about to leave without telling me who they are."
Caption: **97% leave. no name. no trace.**
Action: a ghost gives you a little apologetic wave on the way out. comedic.

0:15 to 0:24
You stand up. Slowly reach off screen. Pull out the laser gun.
"yeah. no."
Caption: **not today.**
Action: deadpan confidence. you cock the laser gun.

0:24 to 0:45 (the transformation, the hero gag)
You fire the laser at the nearest ghost. Hard cut: the sheet drops and a real person is standing there (you, in a wig, or a friend, whatever is funniest), holding their phone, looking at their own x profile.
"oh. it is sarah. founder of a coffee startup. she read my pricing page twice and just posted about hating her current tool."
Captions, one per beat: **see them.** **find them.**
Action: zap the next ghost, another reveal. the gag escalates. each ghost becomes a specific, funny, real seeming person with a tell.

0:45 to 1:05
Back to you, calm, holstering the laser gun.
"that is beam. it shows you who is actually on your site, finds where they post, and writes you a reply that sounds like you. you send it from your own account. on their feed. while they still remember you."
Caption: **say hi back. in your voice.**

1:05 to 1:22
"no setup, no scraping sketchy data, no logging into anything. you paste your url and take a nap. i will beam the rest."
Caption: **paste url. take a nap.**
Action: one ghost is still trying to sneak out behind you. you zap it without looking.

1:22 to 1:30, close and reply bait
"how many ghosts left your site today? be honest." 
Caption: **how many ghosts left your site today?**
Final frame: you blow imaginary smoke off the laser gun, point it at the lens, beam wordmark.

CTA on screen: **beam. private beta. limited spots.** (link in first reply.)

# format 3: the demo speedrun

The native, POV, screen plus face one. Feels like a video a founder shot on their phone, not an ad. Proves the product is real in under a minute. Lowest production lift, highest "wait this is actually useful" energy.

Why it wins: it looks unproduced, which on X reads as honest. Shows the whole loop working live. Best for the people who are already nodding and need proof.

### three second hook
Visual: vertical, your face in a small circle in the corner, the rest of the screen is the live beam dashboard. Phone recording energy.
Line: "someone just bounced off my landing page. watch me find them before they forget i exist."
Caption: **let's catch a stranger in real time.**

### beat sheet (target 80s, the whole thing is short enough to post as is)

0:00 to 0:04, hook above. A new visitor row pings in on the dashboard.

0:04 to 0:18
"okay. new visitor. i do not have their email, they did not sign up, they did nothing. normally this person is gone forever."
You raise the laser gun into frame and tap the row with it.
"beam already pulled who they are. name, company, the page they are on."
Caption: **step one: see them.**

0:18 to 0:35
"now where do they actually hang out. beam matched their x handle. and look, they posted forty minutes ago about exactly the problem i solve."
Caption: **step two: find them.**
Action: you point the laser at the matched profile, it snaps into focus.

0:35 to 0:55
"here is the part that matters. beam already drafted a reply. it is based on their actual post, not some 'hey i noticed you visited' creep message. it sounds like me."
You read the draft out loud, fast. It should be genuinely good and specific.
Caption: **step three: say hi on their feed.**

0:55 to 1:10
"i tweak one word, i hit send from my own account, on native x. that is it. no automation, no bots, nothing that gets me banned. the whole thing took me thirty seconds while i talked to you."
Caption: **30 seconds. your account. their feed.**

1:10 to 1:20, close and reply bait
"this is beam. it is in private beta. what would you say to a visitor if you actually knew who they were? best reply gets an invite."
Caption: **best reply gets an invite.**
Final frame: you spin the laser gun once, point at lens, beam wordmark.

CTA on screen: **beam. see who's into you. say hi back.** (link in first reply. the "best reply gets an invite" line will print replies, lean into it.)

## the posting playbook (this is half the launch)

The video is the asset. The post is the distribution. Do not waste a great video on a lazy post.

### what the two posts you sent actually teach

The supermemory post (589K views) led with the unlock, not the launch: "you can now run supermemory locally. introducing supermemory local," then three bullets of what it does. The TRAE post (8M views, though that account had paid amplification, so treat the number as ceiling not baseline) used a rhythmic parallel tagline: "meet TRAE SOLO Mobile: think anytime, ship everywhere," one capability line, then where to get it.

Three things both did that your caption should steal. They led with capability, not problem, so a stranger understood the product in one second. They were short and scannable, a headline plus bullets or a single line, no story. And both anchored on a clean name line, "introducing" or "meet" plus the product.

My earlier caption led with the problem, which is great for farming replies but slower to repost because people have to read to the end to get what it is. For a launch moment specifically, capability first wins, and beam already owns the perfect parallel tagline. So lead with the unlock, then drop the problem as the second beat, then still end on a question so you keep the reply engine running.

### the main tweet, no link, three options to pick from

Option A, capability led, supermemory structure. "you can now see exactly who is on your website. introducing beam. it finds out who your anonymous visitors are, matches their x handle, and writes you a reply so you say hi on their feed. in 30 seconds. no signup from them, no cold email from you." Then the native video.

Option B, tagline led, TRAE structure. "meet beam: see who's into you, say hi back. it turns your anonymous website traffic into real people you can actually reach, on their own feed, in your own voice. now in private beta." Then the native video.

Option C, problem led, reply bait. "97% of your website visitors leave and you never find out who they were. i got tired of that, so i built beam. it shows you who is on your site, finds their x handle, and drafts the reply so you say hi on their feed in 30 seconds." Then the native video.

My pick: Option A for the launch post because it reposts the fastest, with a reply-bait question pinned as your own first comment underneath so you still farm the thread. No link anywhere in the main tweet.

First reply, the link plus the question. Immediately reply to your own tweet with both the route and the reply bait: "private beta is open, limited spots, i onboard everyone myself. waitlist here, getbeam.fyi. tell me, who is the last website visitor you actually talked to?" The algorithm penalizes the link, not the reply, so this keeps your main post at full reach while still routing people and opening the thread. If you used Option A or B (capability led, no question in the main tweet), this is where the question lives.

Time it for velocity. Post when indie founders are online and scrolling, which is roughly 9 to 11am US eastern on a tuesday, wednesday, or thursday. Avoid weekends for a B2B launch. Then sit on the post for 60 minutes and reply to every single comment. First hour engagement is the strongest signal you have.

Engineer the replies. Every script ends on a question for a reason. When someone answers, do not just like it, reply with a real follow up so the thread keeps moving. Each reply is worth far more than a like to the algorithm.

Push the short cut, not the long one. Pin the 80 to 100 second hero version, but the 35 to 45 second cut is what you post into the feed for reach. Same hook, ends right after the demo and the question.

Repost yourself the next morning with a different hook line and the same video. Not spam, a second at bat. Most of your audience never saw it the first time.

---

## quick recommendation if you only do one thing

Shoot format 1 as your hero. It has the highest reply ceiling, the demo is the proof, and the laser hitting the screen and resolving a visitor is the single best shot in the whole concept. Cut it to 40 seconds for the feed, keep the 85 second version pinned and on your site. Formats 2 and 3 become your week two and week three follow ups so the launch is a campaign, not a single post.
