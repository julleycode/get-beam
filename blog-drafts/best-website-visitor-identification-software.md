# Best Website Visitor Identification Software (2026)

Around 97 percent of your website visitors never fill out a form. Visitor identification software tells you who they were, and in 2026 the category splits into two very different products wearing the same label: company-level tools that name the visiting organization, and person-level tools that name the actual human. Picking the best website visitor identification software starts with knowing which of those two you need.

## 1. Beam: person-level identification plus the outreach

[Beam](https://getbeam.fyi/) identifies the individual people on your site, with a published average of 60 to 80 percent of visitors identified, then closes the loop no one else closes: it auto-matches each person's social profiles across LinkedIn, X, and 10+ platforms and drafts a reply in your voice for you to send from your own account. Setup is one line of HTML, first identified visitor in about 30 minutes, and pricing is flat: free for 10 identified visitors a month, $19 for 50, $49 for unlimited with team seats and API. GDPR and CCPA compliant, data never resold, no automation touching your accounts.

**Skip Beam if:** you run a multi-rep account-based sales machine that needs routing, territories, and orchestration. That's platform territory, and the honest picks there are below.

## 2. RB2B: person-level for US-heavy traffic

RB2B made person-level identification famous and pipes identified visitors into Slack in real time. Its documented limits: person-level resolution works only on US traffic, standard plans identify roughly 5 to 20 percent of it (35 to 45 percent on Pro+), and credit pricing runs $79 to $399+ a month with 25 to 45 cent overages. Strong pick for US SaaS with SDRs; the full breakdown is in our [RB2B alternative guide](https://getbeam.fyi/blog/rb2b-alternative).

## 3. Leadfeeder: the company-level veteran

Part of Dealfront, Leadfeeder matches IPs to organizations, scores accounts, and syncs two-way with CRMs, with strong European coverage and GDPR posture. From €99 a month billed annually (about 40 percent more monthly); the free plan holds 100 companies with 7-day retention. Best for account-based teams; see it head-to-head in [RB2B vs Leadfeeder](https://getbeam.fyi/blog/rb2b-vs-leadfeeder).

## 4. Warmly: identification inside a GTM platform

Warmly wraps company and contact identification in orchestration: automated sequences, AI chat, meeting booking, retargeting. Paid plans start around $15,000 a year, with person-level on-site ID around $30,000. The free tier reveals up to 500 visitors monthly. Right for sales orgs with a RevOps owner; the founder-sized case is in our [Warmly alternative guide](https://getbeam.fyi/blog/warmly-alternative).

## 5. Snitcher: simple company-level, honest pricing

Snitcher does clean company-level identification with transparent tiers from €49 a month for 50 identifications. No enterprise bloat, good for lean teams that just want to see which companies visit. Company-level only, so the "who do I actually contact" work remains yours.

## 6. Leadinfo: the European SMB pick

Leadinfo starts around €39 to €69 a month, with a strong identification rate on European traffic and ISO compliance. A sensible default for EU SMBs that want company names in their CRM without a big contract.

## 7. Albacross: EU ABM with intent data

Albacross layers buying-intent signals on company identification, aimed at European ABM teams. Pricing is custom-quoted, typically starting around $125 a month and rising steeply with contracts. If you need intent scoring across target accounts and have budget to negotiate, it's a real option; if you want published pricing, look elsewhere.

## 8. HubSpot Breeze Intelligence: for HubSpot lifers

The former Clearbit Reveal now lives inside HubSpot: company-level identification on credits from about $45 a month per 100, requiring a paid HubSpot subscription (Sales Hub Pro+ for the visitor features). Fine consolidation if you're all-in on HubSpot; a lock-in problem if you're not. The full story is in our [Clearbit Reveal alternative guide](https://getbeam.fyi/blog/clearbit-reveal-alternative).

## How to Actually Choose

Four questions settle it. Person or company level: direct outreach needs a person; account-based motions can run on companies (the difference is unpacked in our [person-level guide](https://getbeam.fyi/blog/person-level-visitor-identification)). Where's your traffic: US-only person-level tools quietly fail international sites, and EU traffic raises consent requirements. What's your price shape: credits punish growth, flat plans don't, platforms need staffing. And the loop question: a name in a dashboard isn't pipeline until someone writes the message, so check who's doing that work, you or the tool.

Most tools here have free tiers. The best evaluation is boring: install two or three, wait two weeks, count what each identified on your real traffic, and spot-check quality. Vendor benchmarks, including ours, are marketing until your own site confirms them.

## FAQ

**What is the best website visitor identification software?**
There's no universal best. Beam leads for founders wanting person-level ID plus drafted outreach from $0. Leadfeeder leads for account-based EU teams. RB2B leads for US SaaS with SDR teams. Match the tool to your traffic and your sales motion.

**What's the difference between person-level and company-level identification?**
Company-level tools use reverse IP lookup to name the visiting organization. Person-level tools resolve the actual individual via identity graphs. Person-level removes the "who do I contact" step but coverage varies more between vendors.

**Is there free visitor identification software?**
Yes. Beam (10 identified visitors a month, person-level, outreach included), RB2B (150 resolutions, company-level), Leadfeeder (100 companies, 7-day retention), and Warmly (500 revealed visitors) all have free tiers as of July 2026.

**How accurate are these tools?**
Published claims range from 5 to 20 percent (RB2B standard, US person-level) to 60 to 80 percent (Beam's reported average), with company-level tools measuring differently again. Methodology and traffic mix drive the variance, so test on your own site.
