# The Best RB2B Alternative in 2026 (Starts at $0)

If you're searching for an RB2B alternative, you probably hit one of three walls. Your traffic isn't all US-based, so person-level identification stopped at the border. Your credits burned out mid-month and the overage math got uncomfortable. Or you got a list of names and realized RB2B's job ends exactly where your actual work begins: you still have to find each person, research them, and write the outreach yourself.

None of these are dealbreakers for every team. But if any of them made you open a search tab, here is an honest comparison and a look at Beam, the alternative built for founders who want the identification and the outreach in one loop.

**TL;DR:** RB2B is a solid pick if you have high US traffic volume, an SDR team to work the list, and budget for Pro+ where person-level coverage gets meaningful. Beam is the better fit if you're a founder or small team that wants person-level identification plus ready-to-send outreach drafts, starting free and topping out at $49 a month. If raw company-level volume on a free plan is all you need, RB2B's free tier actually gives you more names per month than Beam's.

## What RB2B Does Well

Credit where it's due. RB2B put person-level visitor identification on the map. Adam Robinson built the category's loudest brand on LinkedIn, and the product delivers what made it famous: it tells you which specific people visited your site, not just which companies.

The Slack integration is genuinely slick. Identified visitors drop into a channel in real time, which fits how sales teams already work. The free plan is generous on volume, currently 150 resolutions a month, and setup is a simple script install. For a US-focused SaaS with an outbound team ready to act on every alert, RB2B does its one job well.

## Where RB2B Falls Short

These are the complaints that show up consistently in reviews and comparison research, current as of July 2026.

**Person-level identification is US-only.** RB2B resolves individual identities only when the visitor's IP can be confirmed inside the United States. If a meaningful share of your traffic comes from Europe, Asia, or anywhere else, those visitors stay anonymous or resolve to company names at best.

**Person-level coverage is thinner than the marketing suggests.** Across independent reviews, RB2B identifies roughly 5 to 20 percent of US traffic at the person level on standard plans. The premium resolution waterfall on Pro+ pushes that to a reported 35 to 45 percent, but that tier starts at $199 a month for 600 resolutions.

**Credits burn fast and don't roll over.** Pricing runs from a free company-level tier to Starter at $79 a month for 300 resolutions, Pro at $149 for 600 (scaling to $399 for 2,500), and Pro+ from $199. High-traffic sites hit caps quickly, overages run 25 to 45 cents per resolution, and unused credits vanish at the end of each cycle. Reviewers also flag lead quality issues: bot traffic, invalid emails, and incomplete profiles eating into the credits you paid for.

**It identifies, then stops.** This is the structural one. RB2B hands you a name and a LinkedIn URL. Finding the right way to reach that person, figuring out what they care about, and writing the message is entirely on you. For a sales team with SDRs, that's fine. For a founder doing outreach between product work, it's the difference between a tool you use daily and a Slack channel you eventually mute.

Also worth knowing: a January 2026 update removed contact-level data from RB2B's free tier, so the free plan is now company-level only.

Yes. Beam's free plan includes 10 identified visitors a month at the person level, with LinkedIn and X profiles and AI-drafted outreach included. No card required. RB2B also has a free tier, but since January 2026 it's company-level only.

**Does RB2B work outside the US?**
RB2B's person-level identification only works for US traffic. Visitors from other regions aren't resolved to individuals. If your audience is international, that's the first thing to test in any trial.
